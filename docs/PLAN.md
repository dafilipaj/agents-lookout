Proposed: one scheme, written down once (a Routes table in SIDE's Technical approach), and every view's path built by it: one short prefix per kind (`/s`, `/h`, `/l`, `/t`, `/pr`, `/settings`); a view that belongs to something under its owner (a Session's spec at `/s/<id>/spec/<checkout>/<path>`, a PR's at `/pr/<repo>/<number>/spec/<path>`, Suggest at a path no Topic id can take); sub-pages and tabs as the last segment; a place in a page in the hash. Every old path redirects (`replace`), so shared and bookmarked links still open. A change across SIDE, HIST, LAUNCH, TOPIC and PLAIN, which name the routes (`/topics` alone 27 times), with a Changelog line in each, then `router.ts` and the tests that check paths. To decide when specced: one letter or words for the prefixes, and whether the right sidebar's open tab joins the path.
53. **ARCH: a repo's structural problems, and what a Session's Changes do to them** ([spec](features/architecture/SPEC.md), Draft 2026-11-18). Dario 2026-11-18: "we're not coding by hand anymore and we're not reading code anymore", and his work moved from an IDE to Lookout. He asked whether tracking a Session's changes and an app's architecture is worth adding, from two sides: "when i'm working on a PR in a session", and "when i want to understand architecture and notice any anti-patterns or problems arising", such as a dependency graph showing a "god object". Ranked, it showed one hand-written file of about 12,000 lines that 308 files in 17 of 19 units import, changed in about one commit in ten, plus 46 import cycles and 88 files over 1,000 lines. Drafted as ARCH:

  Next: a grill. Its blocking questions are which slice comes first, the scanner's engine, where a repo's Findings live (a canvas), and the thresholds checked on real repos. The research with its sources, the probe's numbers by file and the probe script are in Dario's `output/lookout-2026-11-18-architecture/`.
54. **PING: Slack threads that ask for you, beside your Sessions** ([spec](features/pings/SPEC.md), Draft 2026-11-18). 55. **What backs a Session's Done** (Dario 2026-11-18: "Read through relevant posts" by Steve Yegge and Addy Osmani "and suggest me top 3-4 features we could add to Lookout based on their insights"; of the four suggested he took three, items 55 to 57). Read the same day: Yegge's ten Medium posts of February to October 2026 and three essays on yegge.ai, and Osmani's 33 posts of 2026. Both come back to one point: starting agents is cheap, and the limit is what you can verify and decide. This item is the theme they weigh most. Osmani: "Every agent-completed task should terminate in concrete evidence: a test that runs, a screenshot, a log, a runtime trace, a reviewer signoff" ([Cognitive Surrender](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-surrender/)). He names "Summary substitution", where "the agent's work summary substitutes for review", as an anti-pattern ([Agentic Autonomy Levels](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agentic-autonomy-levels/)), and gives two rules: "Treat any diff that rewrites many tests as a flag and read those first" ([Agentic Code Review](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agentic-code-review/)), and "Models reliably skew positive when they grade their own work" ([Long-running Agents](https://addyosmani.com/blog/long-running-agents/)). In Yegge's factory the maker never grades itself: one model designs, another implements, the first reviews ([The Shape of Things to Come, Part 1](https://yegge.ai/essays/the-shape-of-things-to-come/)). Today a Done Session's row shows the Done chip and the Session's own note (SIDE-2.17), which is the summary. Its Changes (DIFF), what it produced (LIB) and its PR's CI state (WORK-2.3) are in three places, and nothing says whether a test ran after its last edit. Proposed: a block on a Session, read from its Transcript and its Checkouts and not written by the Session:
    - the test commands it ran, and how each ended;
    - whether a file changed after the last run that passed;
    - how many test files are among its Changes;
    - the pictures and files it produced (LIB);
    - whether another model reviewed the work;
    - its Linked PR's CI state.

  With nothing to show it says so in one line, for example "Done, with no test run after its last edit". What it costs: reading tool calls and their results out of both Harnesses' Transcripts is reusable logic, so a Building block read with its contract written first ([0005](decisions/0005-building-blocks-live-outside-lookout.md)); Codex's Transcripts weren't checked. To decide when specced: what counts as a test run (patterns, or a list per repo); whether a review by another model can be told from a Transcript; where the block lives (the Session's header, its expanded row, the Done chip's tooltip); and whether it joins ARCH-4, which answers the same question, what a Session did, for someone who doesn't read the code.
56. **A Busy Session that is quietly going wrong** (from the same reading as item 55). Osmani calls it the "ambient anxiety tax" of Sessions run in parallel, "the anxiety of not knowing what any one agent might be quietly getting wrong" ([Your parallel Agent limit](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-parallel-agents/)), and gives the sign of a stuck loop: "the same command being tried over and over without any change in the result" ([Practical Loop Engineering](https://addyosmani.com/blog/practical-loop-engineering/)). Yegge builds a role whose only job is to "look for stuff that's simply stuck or dropped", under the rule "crons watch, models act" (The Shape of Things to Come, Part 1), and notes that "idleness means staleness, and clones can fall far behind" ([Fences, not Sandboxes](https://yegge.ai/essays/fences-not-sandboxes/)). Today a Busy row shows Fleet's `activity` text (SIDE-2.11) and the time since its Status changed (SIDE-2.1), and MEM-4.1 marks a Session whose memory grows fast. A Session that repeats a failing command looks like one that makes progress. Proposed: marks on the row, each from a rule with no model, each saying its reason in words:
    - the same command failed three times running;
    - Busy for N minutes with nothing new in its Transcript;
    - one of its changed files is in another live Session's Changes too;
    - its branch is N commits behind its repo's default branch.

  What it costs: the third needs only DIFF's answers, and ARCH-4.6 already lists those files in a Session's view, so one read serves both and one spec should own it. The first two need the Transcript read of item 55. The fourth needs a git read Fleet doesn't give (its `git` holds `branch`, `linked`, `repo` and `root`), which is a commit in the fork ([0006](decisions/0006-run-a-fork-of-fleet.md)). To decide when specced: the thresholds, since a long build or a Queued test run is silent too (SIDE-2.6, RES); which marks earn a place on a quiet row (DS-22); how a false alarm is dismissed; and that a mark never changes the Session's Status, which stays the Harness's word.
57. **How long Sessions wait on you** (from the same reading as item 55). Osmani: "Scale fleet to review rate, not the UI" and "Batch your reviews" ([The Orchestration Tax](https://addyosmani.com/blog/orchestration-tax/)). He puts his own ceiling at "somewhere around three to four threads" and says "Watch your review quality, not your agent count" (Your parallel Agent limit). Yegge: "in the limit, I am the bottleneck" (The Shape of Things to Come, Part 1), and "3 to 4 hours is going to be the sweet spot for the new workday" ([The AI Vampire](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/the-ai-vampire-eda6e4f07163)). Today Sessions that Need you come first (SIDE-3.1), ⌘J selects the next one (SIDE-5.3) and the tab title counts them (SIDE-3.3); the Backlog holds a toast and a count ([Needs you while you look elsewhere](BACKLOG.md#needs-you-while-you-look-elsewhere-a-toast-and-a-count-side)). Nothing records how long a Session waited or how many ran at once. Proposed: Lookout keeps the Status changes it already sees, and shows:
    - a line for today, for example "Peak 7 Sessions. They waited on you 2 h 40 min, the longest 52 min";
    - over weeks, the wait by number of live Sessions: your ceiling, from your own numbers;
    - in the launcher, what already waits before you start another ("4 Sessions wait on you, the oldest 38 min"). It never blocks the start.

  What it costs: Lookout's own store and no Building block, so it's the cheapest of the three. To decide when specced: what ends a wait (your prompt or answer, which Fleet reports as `prompted_at`; for a Done Session, its close or its next prompt); whether time with the Mac asleep or Lookout's server stopped counts; where today's line lives (a footer line, DS-23, or the usage details); how long the history is kept; and whether the launcher's line informs or nags.
